## Черновик ответа в #57 (отправка после релиза 2.7 — №84/№92/№93)

Shipped in KAIF 2.7 (epic HO). You were right twice over, and the second half turned out to be the bigger one.

**The word.** `baton` is gone from the payload — 19 phrases across 9 files (`/end-chat-soft`, `/end-chat-force`, `/pause`,
`/nightloop`, `/team-deployment` and its two reference templates, `KAIF_REFERENCE`, the hooks module) now say *handover*:
"record the handover", "STATUS.md is the handover between sessions", "no closure without the handover". And the rule
behind it went into `AGENT_GUIDE` (both layers), under "Languages": **a term that turns absurd in the owner's language is
checked against that skill's own trigger aliases**. That is not a style note — it is where the answer already was. The
Russian aliases of `/end-chat-soft` had said *pass the relay* since 2.2, while the canon those aliases trigger said
*baton*: the guide was arguing with its own triggers, and nobody noticed until you read it. Three steps now: grep the
skill's aliases (an alias that names the thing IS the canonical translation — never coin a second one beside it), prefer
the industry's word to a private one, and read the translation aloud once. A pair check keeps the rule honest — it fails
if the Russian pack stops carrying the alias the rule cites.

**The mechanism — this is the part your ticket bought.** A module is addressed by its SIGNATURE: the full heading line.
So renaming a heading looked, to `update`, exactly like "the old module was removed and a new one was added". Before
touching the code I wrote an executable probe across the three merge branches, and the measurement corrected my own plan:
an UNTOUCHED module landed correctly by accident (removed, then re-inserted), a module the owner had EDITED got **two
sections with the same content** — silently, at exit 0 — and neither branch ever said the word "renamed", so you could
not tell a rename from a delete-plus-add even when the result was right.

Fixed by declaration, not by guessing — the same answer every migration system gives to this ambiguity (Django's
autodetector refuses to guess a rename and asks the developer; Liquibase gives `renameColumn` its own change type). The
bundle meta now ships `renamesByVersion`, applied over the `(from, to]` interval like policy changes, and `update` treats
a declared pair as ONE module: untouched → replaced under the new heading; carrying your edits → your text stays, with
ONE heading and a task item that names the rename and carries the diff; an old anchor that is not on disk → a log line by
name, never a failure. Every outcome is logged as `renamed: <path> :: <old> → <new>`. Nothing is matched by similarity.
And because an undeclared rename is still a duplicate, the BUILD now warns by name when a heading vanishes from a
template with neither a rename nor a deprecation behind it — the class is guarded from the side where it is born.

Two things for your tree when you update: if it already carries both sections from an earlier update, fold them by hand
once (the machinery will not guess which half is yours), and replace the old word in your own STATUS or chronicle by
hand — owner documents are never edited by the machinery. Both are stated in the update task.

Proof: suite `s27` — 15 checks green on 2.7, and red on the 2.6 core (`KAIF_DIST` → `git show v2.6:dist/…`): "5 of 15",
the duplicate plus three silent logs plus the missing map, reaching a verdict rather than crashing; the build guard
proven red on the real path by removing one declared pair and watching it name that exact heading; the full sandbox
polygon "all 26 suites green"; the origin's own run is filed as `testcases/reports/2026-09-12_polygon-2.7-HO.md`.

Thank you — "What does Baton mean?" was the cheapest possible phrasing of a defect that had two layers.
